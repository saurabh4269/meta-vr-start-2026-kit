# Demo-video trailer scripts: all six ideas

One trailer script per idea in [idea-shortlist.md](../research/idea-shortlist.md), modelled on the 2025 Best Casual Game winner's 46-second trailer (hook, montage, second feature, tagline).

- Every feature shown comes from the shortlist. Anything else is marked ⚠.
- **Hard limit:** the contest video must be under 3 minutes. These run 60–70 s.
- **Sadak VR** is on hold until Saurabh says go. Its script is ready but not active.

---

## Shared recipe (applies to all six)

**Structure (from the Tiny Golf trailer)**
1. **Hook (0–6 s):** one simple action a stranger understands in two seconds, with hands in frame.
2. **Montage (4–6 short cuts, 6–10 s each):** moments only MR or VR can do. Put the awe moment in the middle, not at the end.
3. **Second feature (about 10 s):** the thing that makes people come back or play with others.
4. **Tagline and name card (6–8 s):** one line, then the app's own name. Nothing else on the card.

**Footage**
- First-person only, no third-person shots. Your own hands are in frame in every shot.
- Move your head slowly. You are the camera operator.
- **Mixed reality (MR):** a clean, bright, uncluttered room or desk. Use the passthrough in the Quest 3/3S.
- **Capture, pick one:**
  - **Headset Camera app:** Landscape, 1920×1080, stabilization Low. Pull files with `adb pull`. Works on Linux.
  - **MQDH Cast:** Cinematic 16:9, 60 fps (Windows). Don't use the cropped 16:9 option, because it cuts hands off.
- Record 3 takes of each beat. Keep the best, and keep each clip short.
- Simulator footage is allowed, but MR beats look far better from a real Quest 3/3S.

**Voiceover and audio**
- Record the voiceover separately (phone or Audacity, in a quiet room). Calm, plain, not rushed.
- **Budget:** about 2 words per second, with gaps. Every beat below is written to fit.
- Quiet royalty-free music underneath. Keep the licence file.
- No big text over the gameplay. Only English subtitles for speech (any non-English line gets subtitles).

**Rules checklist before upload**
- [ ] Hands only (no controllers in any shot)
- [ ] No logos, brands or recognisable branded products: shoes, bottles, laptops, posters, the IITB crest, other app icons, brand names in code or paper. Cover or turn them away. Use plain items.
- [ ] No identifiable real people (use stylised avatars if anyone appears)
- [ ] No third-party names or marks in the app's title or on the end card
- [ ] Under 3 minutes, English or English subtitles
- [ ] **Public** on YouTube (not unlisted). Check the link in an incognito window.
- [ ] Credits and licences go in the video description, as text

## 1. Hands of a Master

**Target length:** 60 s

**Hook:** "If you can hold a pen, you can learn from a master."

| Time | Shot | On-screen action | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:06 | First-person MR, a plain desk with one sheet of paper, close on the writing hand holding a brush pen | Translucent gold-tinted ghost hands fade in over your own hand and the page | "If you can hold a pen, you can learn from a master." |
| 0:06–0:14 | First-person MR, hands-and-page framing, from slightly above | The ghost hands write one calligraphy stroke at full speed, leaving a faint trail on the real paper | "A master's hands appear over your real paper, and write one stroke." |
| 0:14–0:22 | Same framing; your hand follows. Last two seconds: close on your other hand | You shadow the ghost at a slowed tempo. Your free hand does a two-finger spread and the ghost slows further | "Spread two fingers to slow them down. Then follow along." |
| 0:22–0:32 | Close, hand-level, the hero shot | Your hand slides into the ghost hand and the two lock together. The stroke glows gold on your real page | "Slide your hand into theirs. For one stroke, you move as the master." |
| 0:32–0:40 | First-person MR, a floating score card beside the page | A per-stroke score appears: path, order, speed | "Every stroke is scored: path, order, speed." |
| 0:40–0:48 | First-person MR, two quick cuts: replay, then pause | You pinch to scrub and replay the master's stroke. You hold your palm up and the ghost freezes | "Pinch to replay. Palm up to pause. Go at your own pace." |
| 0:48–0:55 | First-person MR, lesson cards floating above the desk | You poke a card to pass to the next stroke set. A practice-streak counter ticks up | "Pass a lesson, unlock the next. Come back daily to keep your streak." |
| 0:55–1:00 | Clean card (end card) over a still of the desk | Tagline then the name | "Calligraphy first. Pottery and sitar are next. Hands of a Master." |

**Total VO:** 90 words, 60 s.

**Prep and capture**
- **Mode:** mixed reality, entirely.
- **Room and props:** a matte desk with good, even light. Plain white or cream paper with a faint stroke grid, a plain brush pen (no brand text), and no clutter.
- **Capture:** headset Camera app, 1920×1080 landscape. Use the Camera app for the close hero shot so the hands stay sharp.
- Record the master sequence as a real take, not CG. Film the "slide into ghost hand" shot at least five times.
- If the pen occludes your fingers badly, film the "air pen" fingertip mode (shortlist fallback) instead and keep the line "hold a pen" out of the hook.

**Must build first to film this**
1. MRUK desk plane anchoring and one marked page.
2. One recorded master sequence (a short character set), played back as translucent ghost hands.
3. Slow-tempo mode, with the two-finger spread control.
4. Hand-lock moment and the gold glow on the stroke.
5. Per-stroke scoring card (path, order, speed), scored loosely with smoothing.
6. Palm-up pause and pinch scrub.
7. Lesson cards and a streak counter (can be a simple fake-day counter for filming ⚠ label it honestly in the description).

Pottery and sitar are shown only as words on the end line, not as footage.

## 2. Mahabharat Chausar

**Target length:** 62 s

**Hook:** "Win at dice. Cheat if you dare."

| Time | Shot | On-screen action | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:06 | First-person MR, an empty real table, hands resting in frame | A carved cross-shaped chausar board rises out of the table surface | "A carved chausar board rises out of your table." |
| 0:06–0:14 | First-person MR, low and close on the table | You throw the dice with a real throw. They tumble and bounce on the board | "Throw the dice with a real throw. Your hand decides the roll." |
| 0:14–0:22 | First-person MR, looking across the table | You pinch-grab a piece and move it along the track. The AI Shakuni sits opposite, watching | "Pinch your pieces around the cross. Across the table, Shakuni is watching." |
| 0:22–0:30 | Close on your hand | A held pinch before release, then a wrist flick: the dice are loaded. A small karma meter drops | "Want to win? Load the dice. A secret flick of the wrist. It costs you karma." |
| 0:30–0:38 | First-person MR, Shakuni in frame, then your hand | Shakuni's hand points: "Cheat!" | "But Shakuni watches your hands. And he can call you out." |
| 0:38–0:48 | Wide on the board from your seat, hero shot | Caught: your side of the board cracks open, divine light pours out and the true roll is shown. Your pieces are removed | "Get caught, and your side of the board cracks open. Light pours through, and shows the true roll." |
| 0:48–0:56 | First-person MR, board and table | Cheat well, and the board erupts into a short Kurukshetra battle on the table | "Cheat well, and the board becomes Kurukshetra." |
| 0:56–1:02 | Clean card (end card) | Tagline then the name | "Throw. Cheat. Don't get caught. Mahabharat Chausar." |

**Total VO:** 92 words, 62 s.

**Prep and capture**
- **Mode:** mixed reality for the whole film (the board lives on your real table).
- **Room and props:** a plain, light-coloured table with nothing on it. No cloth with patterns or brands. Dim the room slightly so the divine light reads well.
- **Capture:** headset Camera app, 1920×1080 landscape. Film the dice throws more than once. Fast throws can leave the tracking volume, so keep takes where tracking held.
- Shakuni is original, respectful art, not a likeness of anyone. Story material is from the public-domain Mahabharata only.

**Must build first to film this**
1. A board that spawns on a real table plane (MRUK).
2. Throw physics from real hand motion, with last-good-pose handling.
3. Pinch-grab pieces and the cross-shaped track rules (enough to play a few turns).
4. The AI Shakuni opponent (1v1, no networking), including the "Cheat!" call.
5. Secret loading gestures (held pinch, flick) with a karma meter.
6. The cracking-board "caught" effect with the divine light and true-roll reveal.
7. A short scripted Kurukshetra spectacle, not a full battle.

## 3. Artemis Flyby Chair

The working title on screen is **Flyby Chair**, with no NASA or Artemis names, logos or patches (rule 4(19)). ⚠ Check NASA's media guidelines before using any real lunar imagery.

**Target length:** 70 s

**Hook:** "Sit down. Your chair is the commander's seat."

| Time | Shot | On-screen action | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:07 | First-person MR, sitting in a real chair in a clean room, hands on knees | The capsule cabin fades in around the chair and the room walls dissolve | "Sit down. Your chair is now the commander's seat." |
| 0:07–0:15 | First-person, hands on the panel | You poke toggle switches. The cabin shakes lightly. Countdown | "Press the switches. Launch in three, two, one." |
| 0:15–0:25 | First-person VR, at the porthole | Earth shrinks outside. You hold it between two fingers | "Out the window, Earth gets smaller. Soon it fits between your fingers." |
| 0:25–0:33 | First-person VR, porthole | Behind the Moon: radio silence over the lunar surface | "Behind the Moon, the radio goes quiet." |
| 0:33–0:43 | First-person VR, hero shot | Earthrise. You hold up your hand and Earth sits between thumb and finger | "Then Earth rises. And you can hold it in your hand." |
| 0:43–0:53 | First-person VR, the panel | The crewmate talks you through a fix. You twist a valve and reseat a breaker | "A crewmate talks you through the repairs. Twist a valve. Reseat a breaker." |
| 0:53–1:03 | First-person VR, then MR | Reentry glow, splashdown, and the capsule walls dissolve back into your room | "Then reentry. Splashdown. And the walls fade back to your room." |
| 1:03–1:10 | Clean card (end card) | Tagline then the name | "A trip around the Moon, from your own chair. Flyby Chair." |

**Total VO:** 82 words, 70 s.

**Prep and capture**
- **Mode:** mostly fully virtual (cabin and space), with MR only for the first and last seconds, where the room appears through passthrough.
- **Room and props:** an ordinary, uncluttered chair against a plain wall, with no logos on the chair or clothes. Wear a plain top.
- **Capture:** headset Camera app, 1920×1080 landscape. It records the virtual scene as well as MR. Do the first and last beats in a single take so the dissolve in and out is real.
- Keep the shake mild. Don't film the 8–10 minute mission, only the highlights.

**Must build first to film this**
1. Seated chair-anchored cabin that fades in over passthrough.
2. Panel with toggle switches and rotary knobs.
3. The shrinking Earth and the pinch-and-hold scale illusion at the porthole.
4. The far-side silence beat and Earthrise (polish these most).
5. The AI crewmate with a few panel tasks (valve, breaker). Reply cards as the hands-only path.
6. Reentry glow and the walls dissolving back to passthrough.
7. Skybox and textured spheres, to hold 60 fps.

## 4. Code as Architecture

**Target length:** 62 s

**Hook:** "This is code I wrote last night. Now it's a city."

| Time | Shot | On-screen action | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:07 | First-person MR, hands holding a floating panel of real source code over a desk | The panel shows the real source file, in large readable type | "This is code I wrote last night." |
| 0:07–0:15 | First-person MR, the desk from above | The panel folds away and a city rises on the desk: buildings, districts, streets | "Now it's a city on my desk. Files are buildings. Folders are districts." |
| 0:15–0:23 | First-person MR, both hands in frame | A two-hand pinch zooms the city. Taller buildings are the bigger files | "The taller the building, the bigger the file." |
| 0:23–0:34 | First-person MR, close, hero shot | You pinch a building and lift its roof. Floors are functions, the code shows on a panel. One floor smoulders | "Lift the roof. Each floor is a function. One of them is on fire." |
| 0:34–0:42 | First-person MR, close on your hand | Poke the fire and the failing assertion appears | "A failing test is a fire. Poke it to see why." |
| 0:42–0:49 | First-person MR, along a street | You trace a dependency street with one finger to follow the import | "Trace a street to follow an import." |
| 0:49–0:56 | First-person MR, the guide beside a district | You grab the AI guide and place it by a district, and it explains what the module does (captioned) | "Ask the guide what a module does." |
| 0:56–1:02 | First-person MR, wide city | Tests run, the fires go out one by one. Clean card with the name | "Run the tests, and the fires go out. Code as Architecture." |

**Total VO:** 78 words, 62 s.

**Prep and capture**
- **Mode:** mixed reality on a desk.
- **Room and props:** a clear desk, plain wall. Use your own small repository, with no third-party brand names in file names, comments or imports on the shot panels. ⚠ Avoid showing any logos, editor themes with brand marks, or private code.
- **Capture:** MQDH Cast in Cinematic 16:9 is best if you have Windows, because the code text is sharper. Otherwise the Camera app at 1920×1080. Film panels from up close so the text is readable.
- The video should lead with real source files (beat 1) so it doesn't read as another mind-map.

**Must build first to film this**
1. A pre-processed repo JSON (a small CLI or GitHub API step), and instanced boxes for a few hundred files.
2. City layout: files as buildings (height by size or complexity), folders as districts, dependency streets.
3. Pinch-to-open building with floors as functions and a code panel with large type.
4. Two-hand pinch zoom.
5. Fire state for a failing test, with the failing assertion on poke, and fires going out as tests run (can be replayed from a recorded run ⚠).
6. A simple AI guide that answers "what does this module do?", with captions.
7. Street tracing for imports.

## 6. Giants & Hiders

Use no Jet Lag names, logos or assets anywhere (rule 4(19)).

**Target length:** 62 s

**Hook:** "Hide and seek. The seekers are giants."

| Time | Shot | On-screen action | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:07 | First-person MR, hands over a real table, a small city on it | A tiny figure runs down a street while your hand chases it | "Hide and seek. The seekers are giants." |
| 0:07–0:14 | First-person MR, from above the city | The city from real map data sits on your table, one small district | "A city the size of your table. You're the giant." |
| 0:14–0:22 | First-person MR, close on your finger | You draw a circle with your finger. Areas outside it go dark | "Draw a circle to ask: are you in here? Wrong areas go dark." |
| 0:22–0:30 | First-person MR, hand over the map | You move a hand and the map shows hotter or colder | "Move your hand. Hotter. Colder." |
| 0:30–0:40 | First-person VR/MR at street level, hero shot | The tiny hider looks up and sees giant faces peering over the rooftops | "And if you're the hider, you look up, and giants peer over the rooftops." |
| 0:40–0:48 | First-person at street level, then table | The hider answers a photo question with a finger camera-frame gesture and plays curses: fog, scrambled street names | "Hiders fight back. Fog over the map. Scrambled street names." |
| 0:48–0:56 | First-person MR, close | Your fingers close in and pinch the tiny hider out of the city | "Close in, and pinch the hider out of the city." |
| 0:56–1:02 | Clean card (end card) | Tagline then the name | "Swap roles. Go again. Giants & Hiders." |

**Total VO:** 76 words, 62 s.

**Prep and capture**
- **Mode:** mixed reality on a table. The street-level beat (beat 5) is a first-person virtual shot inside the diorama.
- **Room and props:** a plain table, plain wall, well lit. No branded items.
- **Capture:** headset Camera app, 1920×1080 landscape. Build and film the **solo AI hider** first. Beat 5's giant faces need a second player, so film it with a second headset or a stylised stand-in giant (not a real person's face). ⚠ If you can't, cut beat 5 and lengthen beat 6.
- The first seconds must show the hide-and-chase loop (the shortlist's video rule). Don't open with a menu.
- Credit OpenStreetMap (ODbL) in the description.

**Must build first to film this**
1. One small OSM-based city district diorama, held at 60 fps.
2. The hider avatar that runs and can be pinched out of the city.
3. The AI hider (solo mode).
4. The radius-circle question with excluded areas going dark.
5. The thermometer (hotter and colder).
6. Hider-side curses: fog, scrambled street names (freeze-a-giant's-hand is optional).
7. The finger camera-frame photo gesture, with tolerant detection ⚠.
8. For beat 5 only: the street-level hider view with giant faces, which needs a second player or a simple stand-in.
